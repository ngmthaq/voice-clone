# Title: Plan — Mute mic bumps (hand taps, setting the mic down)

- Classification: feature
- Description: Add a bump detector to the noise gate that keeps it closed for short, loud, low-pitched bursts, with a "Block bumps" switch on the Advanced tab.

---

## Approach Summary / Goal

- **Why it happens:** a hand tap or setting the mic on the table makes a short, very loud burst with most of its energy below ~250 Hz. The gate opens for anything loud, and the "Below my voice" check only blocks sounds that are *quieter* than your voice. So the burst goes through the voice model, and you hear it as a loud low boom. Nothing limits the output volume.
- **Fix:** the gate looks at each 20 ms frame. It calls a frame a **bump** when three things are true at once:
  1. The frame starts suddenly: much louder than the frame before it.
  2. It is mostly low-pitched: most of its energy is below ~250 Hz.
  3. It is clearly louder than your learned voice level. If no voice level is learned yet, only rules 1 and 2 apply.

  A bump frame, and a short time after it (~150 ms), keeps the gate closed, even in the middle of speech. Bumps also don't start the gate's hold time, and they don't change the learned voice level.
- **Goal:** taps and table bumps are silent in the output. Normal speech, including low male voices, nasal sounds like "m" and "n", and plosives like "p" and "b", passes as before.

## Functional Requirements

- `GateSettings` gets `block_bumps: bool = True`. When it is `False`, the gate works exactly as it does today.
- A synthetic bump (a short, loud, low-pitched burst like a tap) is muted. Its output level is at least 20 dB lower than its input. This holds both in silence and right after speech.
- Bumps don't raise the learned voice level, and they don't start the gate's hold time.
- Voiced speech with a low pitch (~100 Hz), nasal-like sounds, and a short broadband plosive at a word start pass with the same output as when `block_bumps=False`.
- New setting `bump_filter: bool = True` in `Settings`, saved in `settings.toml`. Presets don't change it. "Reset Advanced settings" sets it to `True`.
- A "Block bumps" `Switch` on the Advanced tab, next to "Noise filter". `ChangerController.set_bump_filter(on)` saves the setting and sends it to a running pipeline through `ConversionPipeline.set_gate_settings`.
- `docs/USAGE.md` gets a "Block bumps" row in the noise gate table, and the note about presets and Reset covers it too.

## Non-Functional Requirements

- numpy only, no new dependency. The per-frame check is cheap (one small FFT per 20 ms frame). It adds no delay and runs in the worker thread, not in the audio callback.
- No Textual code in `domain/` or `controllers/`. Follows `CODING_CONVENTIONS.md` and the no-redundant-comments rule. `pytest` and `ruff check src tests` pass.

## Risks & Assumptions

- Assumption: the thresholds (sudden-start jump, share below 250 Hz, how far above the voice level) are tuned on synthetic signals. A very loud, sudden low word, like a shouted "BOOM", could be cut. The switch lets you turn the detector off.
- Assumption: the gate sees audio after the hum filter (90 Hz), so the detector looks at roughly 90–250 Hz energy.
- Risk: a soft tap that is not louder than your voice won't count as a bump. Only loud bumps are muted.
- Risk: `select_preset` rebuilds `GateSettings` from preset values, which would reset `block_bumps`. The controller must keep the user's choice.
- Out of scope: an output limiter. You chose to mute bumps only.
