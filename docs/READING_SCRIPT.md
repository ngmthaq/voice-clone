# Voice Recording Script (about 20 minutes)

Read this script aloud while you record your voice for training. Every part is written out for you. There is nothing to make up.

How to read:
- Read at your normal speed, in your normal voice, unless a note says otherwise.
- Notes in brackets, like (happy) or (louder), tell you how to say the next lines. Do not read the notes aloud.
- Pause 1–2 seconds between paragraphs. Mistakes are fine: pause, then read the sentence again.
- Keep the same distance from the mic (about one hand width). Do not move closer for quiet parts.
- Take a short break between parts if you need one. Drink some water.

The script covers many sounds, numbers, questions, feelings, and your full voice range, so the model can learn how you sound in every situation.

---

## Part 1 — Introduce yourself

Hello, and thank you for listening. My name is Sam, and I am recording this to teach a computer how my voice sounds.

I live in a busy city, in a small apartment on the fourth floor. From my window, I can see a park, a bakery, and a bus stop where people wait every morning.

I work as a software developer. Most days, I write code, fix problems, and talk with my team about what to build next. I like my job, because every day brings a new puzzle.

I was born in a quiet town near the sea. When I was young, I spent my summers swimming, fishing with my uncle, and reading comic books under a big tree.

I speak English at work and my own language at home. Sometimes I mix them up, and my friends laugh at me.

People say I am calm, patient, and a little too quiet at parties. That is probably true. I prefer small groups and long talks over loud music.

That is enough about me for now. Let us move on to the next part.

## Part 2 — Daily report

Good morning, everyone. Here is my report for Tuesday, the twelfth of March.

Yesterday, I finished the login page and sent it for review. I also fixed two small bugs in the settings screen. One was a wrong label, and the other was a button that did nothing.

Today, I plan to start on the export feature. First, I will read the design notes. Then I will write the code for saving files as PDF and as CSV. If everything goes well, I will have a first version by four thirty this afternoon.

I have one blocker. The test server has been down since eight fifteen this morning, so I cannot check my changes. I have already asked the operations team for help, and they said it should be back within an hour.

A few numbers from last week: we closed forty-seven tickets, opened thirty-one new ones, and the app crashed only twice, down from nine the week before. That is a big improvement.

My next day off is Friday, the fifteenth. Please send me anything urgent before Thursday evening.

That is all from me. Does anyone have questions?

## Part 3 — Feelings

(happy) Guess what? I passed the exam! I studied for three months, and it finally paid off. I am so, so happy right now!

(excited) The concert is tonight! I have been waiting for this all year. I cannot believe we are going to see them live!

(sad) I just heard that our old neighbour passed away. She was always kind to me. I am going to miss her very much.

(tired) I am exhausted. I worked until midnight, and then the baby next door cried until three. I just want to sleep.

(angry, but controlled) No. This is the third time the delivery is late. I paid for next-day shipping, and I want a real answer, not another excuse.

(surprised) Wait, what? You are moving to Canada? Since when? Why did you not tell me earlier?

(worried) The doctor has not called back yet. It has been two days. What if something is wrong?

(nervous) Okay. Deep breath. In five minutes, I have to speak in front of two hundred people. My hands are shaking a little.

(relieved) Oh, thank goodness. The results are fine. Everything is normal. I can finally relax.

(disappointed) I really thought I would get the job. I prepared so well. Oh well, maybe next time.

(proud) Look at this. My daughter built this robot all by herself. She is only ten years old!

(bored) Another meeting about the meeting. We have talked about the same slide for forty minutes.

(grateful) Thank you, truly. You did not have to help me, but you did, and I will not forget it.

(calm) It is all right. Sit down for a moment. We will solve this together, one step at a time.

## Part 4 — Range

(softer) The baby is finally asleep. Please close the door gently and keep your voice down.

(softer) Can you hear the rain on the roof? It sounds so peaceful tonight.

(louder) Hello! Can everyone at the back of the room hear me clearly?

(louder) Hey! Stop! You dropped your wallet!

(higher) Oh, look at that tiny puppy! It is so cute! Can I hold it?

(higher) Really? For me? You remembered my birthday?

(lower) Now my voice is low and slow, like the narrator of a long, dark film about the ocean.

(lower) In the beginning, there was only silence, and the endless cold of space.

(faster) Quick, quick, the train leaves in two minutes, grab your bag, your keys, your phone, and let us go!

(slower) Take your time. Read each word. Feel the weight of every single sound.

(from soft to loud) One. Two. Three. Four. Five. Six. Seven. Eight. Nine. Ten!

(from high to low) Up in the sky, over the hills, down to the valley, and deep into the ground.

(normal again) Now I am back to my everyday voice, for the rest of this script.

## Part 5 — Short story: the turtle and the rabbit

Once upon a time, in a green valley full of flowers, there lived a rabbit who was very fast, and very proud of it.

Every day, the rabbit ran around the valley, shouting, "Nobody is faster than me! Nobody can beat me!"

One morning, he saw a turtle walking slowly along the path. "Look at you," the rabbit laughed. "You are so slow. By the time you reach the river, the summer will be over!"

The turtle stopped and looked at him calmly. "Maybe I am slow," she said. "But I think I can beat you in a race."

The rabbit laughed so hard that he fell over. "You? Beat me? All right, let us race to the old oak tree on the hill. Tomorrow, at sunrise."

The next morning, all the animals came to watch. The owl counted, "Three, two, one, go!"

The rabbit shot forward like an arrow. In a few seconds, he could not even see the turtle behind him. "This is too easy," he thought. "I have plenty of time. I will rest under this bush for a while."

The sun was warm, the grass was soft, and soon the rabbit was fast asleep.

Meanwhile, the turtle kept walking. Step by step. She did not stop to eat. She did not stop to rest. She did not even look back.

Hours later, the rabbit woke up with a jump. "Oh no! What time is it?" He ran up the hill as fast as he could.

But when he got to the top, he saw the turtle, sitting quietly under the old oak tree. The animals were cheering. "She won! The turtle won!"

The rabbit could not believe it. "How?" he asked. "How did you beat me?"

The turtle smiled. "You were faster," she said. "But I never stopped."

And from that day on, the rabbit never laughed at anyone again. Slow and steady wins the race.

## Part 6 — Advanced paragraph

Throughout history, curiosity has been one of humanity's most powerful and most underestimated qualities. Long before the invention of the microscope or the telescope, people looked at the night sky and wondered why the stars moved in such precise, predictable patterns.

Those early observations eventually led to remarkable achievements in astronomy, navigation, and mathematics. Sailors crossed enormous oceans by measuring the angle of the sun at noon, and architects designed cathedrals whose proportions still astonish engineers today.

Nevertheless, progress was rarely straightforward. Brilliant ideas were often ignored, misunderstood, or even deliberately suppressed, simply because they challenged comfortable assumptions. Several scientists spent decades defending theories that are now taught to every schoolchild.

Perhaps the most valuable lesson is this: certainty is not the same as truth. A thoughtful person remains sceptical, but not cynical; open-minded, but not gullible. They ask difficult questions, examine the evidence carefully, and are willing to change their opinion when the facts demand it.

In an age of instant information and endless distraction, that kind of patient, disciplined thinking is more necessary than ever.

## Part 7 — Technology paragraph

Most modern applications run in the cloud, which simply means they run on powerful computers in large data centres instead of on your own laptop. When you open a website, your browser sends a request across the internet, and a server sends back a response, usually in less than a second.

Behind that simple exchange, a lot is happening. The request may pass through a load balancer, which spreads traffic across dozens of servers. A database stores your account details, and a cache keeps popular data in memory so it can be returned quickly.

Security is just as important as speed. Passwords should never be stored as plain text. Instead, they are hashed, so even the developers cannot read them. Connections are encrypted with HTTPS, and important actions often need a second step, such as a code sent to your phone.

Artificial intelligence adds another layer. A machine learning model is trained on thousands or millions of examples until it learns useful patterns. Voice models, for example, learn pitch, rhythm, and tone from recordings like this one.

These tools are powerful, but they must be used responsibly. A cloned voice should only be used with permission, and never to trick, threaten, or deceive another person.

## Part 8 — Demo a feature

Hi everyone. Today I am going to show you the new voice changer screen. It only takes about two minutes.

First, open the app and choose "Voice changer" on the start screen. On this screen, you will see a few drop-down menus.

In the first one, pick your microphone. In my case, it is the built-in microphone. In the second one, pick the output device. On a Mac, that is BlackHole two channel. On Windows, it is CABLE Input.

In the third menu, choose a voice. I will pick the voice I trained last week, called "my voice."

Now, press the Start button. From this moment, everything I say goes through my new voice. The delay is about four hundred milliseconds, so you will hear it almost at once.

Next, let me show you the pitch control. If I move it up by three steps, my voice sounds higher. If I move it down by three, it sounds deeper. Zero means no change.

Finally, there is the Performance menu. If your computer is slow and the voice starts to break up, choose a lighter preset, like "Light" or "Minimal."

When you are finished, press Stop. And that is it. Any questions so far?

## Part 9 — Explain a bug

Let me explain a bug we found last week, and how we fixed it.

Some users said that after talking for a minute or two, their converted voice started to sound strange. It became deep, rough, and robotic, almost like a broken radio.

At first, we thought the problem was the voice model. We tried three different models, but the problem stayed the same. So the model was not the cause.

Then we looked at the noise filter. This filter listens to the background, learns what the noise sounds like, and removes it. The idea is simple and usually works well.

However, we discovered that during long speech, the filter slowly started to treat the voice itself as noise. Because the person never paused, the filter's idea of "background noise" kept rising, until it was cutting away the high parts of the voice.

Without those high frequencies, the model received a dull, muffled voice, and it turned that into the deep, robotic sound that users heard.

The fix had two parts. First, while someone is speaking, the noise level now rises only very slowly. Second, the filter checks the quietest moment in the last two seconds, so it can still notice when the room really does get louder.

After the fix, we tested with ten minutes of non-stop speech, and the voice stayed clear the whole time. We also added automatic tests, so this bug cannot quietly come back.

## Part 10 — Talk about your hobbies

Outside of work, I have three main hobbies: cooking, cycling, and playing the guitar.

Cooking is how I relax. On Sunday afternoons, I put on some music and make something slow, like a beef stew, a big pot of curry, or fresh bread. My favourite part is the smell that fills the whole apartment.

Last month, I tried to make Japanese ramen from scratch. It took eight hours, two failed attempts, and far too many dirty dishes. But the third bowl was honestly the best soup I have ever made.

Cycling keeps me healthy. Every Saturday morning, I ride about forty kilometres along the river with a small group of friends. We stop halfway at a little café that sells the best cinnamon rolls in town.

In winter, when it is too cold or too wet to ride, I practise the guitar instead. I am not very good yet. I can play about fifteen songs, mostly simple ones, but I am slowly learning to play the blues.

One day, I would like to combine all three: ride my bike to a beach, cook dinner over a small fire, and play the guitar while the sun goes down.

That would be a perfect day.

Thank you for listening. This is the end of the recording.
